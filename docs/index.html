Local-first transcript audio editing

Your best take. A clearer workspace.

ScriptSurgeon edits audio by editing its transcript: delete a word and the recording closes the gap. It runs on your machine, ships a document-first workspace with retake review, a fuller recorder, and precise exports. The source is MIT. Installer releases may trail the latest source.

Against a cloud transcript editor

The comparison that matters is not feature count. It is where your recording goes, what it costs over time, and whether you can still open the project in five years.

Consideration ScriptSurgeon Typical cloud editor
Where your audio goes Stays on your disk Uploaded to their servers
Cost over a year Nothing Per seat, per minute, or both
Works offline Yes, entirely No
Cleanup behavior Previewed, never automatic Often applied on import
If the company folds You still have the source Projects go with it
Hand off to an editor EDL and Final Cut XML Usually a rendered file

Verifiable, not just stated

No cloud.
No compromises.

The desktop shell is fenced A new random session token guards the local API, and only trusted loopback hosts are accepted.
Logs stay on the machine Diagnostics are local files, and session tokens are redacted before anything is written.
Checksums on every build A SHA-256 for each package is published beside it on the release page.
Nothing hidden behind a tier No license check exists in the code, because there is nothing to unlock.
